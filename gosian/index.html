Revenue versus cost, by department, in one place

At Gosian Media I built the tracking system, the database and the cross-training model that let leadership see which departments earn their cost, and move people to where they add the most.

Role
Chief Revenue Officer and Data Engineer
Since
June 2025
Team
80 employees
Stack
PostgreSQL on AWS RDS, Python, Docker, dbt

The problem

Money in and money out were not tracked per department. Operational data lived in more than 30 spreadsheets, and keeping them current cost over 10 hours of manual data entry every week. Each department was staffed for one job, so a quiet department still carried its full cost.

What I built

A reporting path from the spreadsheets to one department view, with checks at each step:

  1. Spreadsheets More than 30 files, migrated and retired as the source of truth.
  2. Python and Docker Load scripts with validation, so a bad file is rejected instead of loaded.
  3. PostgreSQL on AWS RDS One relational schema in place of the spreadsheets.
  4. dbt models and tests Revenue and cost by department, with tests on the transformations.
  5. Department view Revenue coming in versus cost going out, per department, against its KPIs.

This is a simplified outline of the flow, not a diagram of the production system.

What I did with the data

Once each department showed its own revenue against its own cost, I could see which ones were strong on their KPIs and which had spare capacity. I cross-trained flexible staff to cover two roles, so capacity followed performance instead of staying fixed. I also recruited and trained more than 30 people myself and wrote the SOPs and training manuals that standardize onboarding.

How to read these numbers

These figures are reported by the company and were not independently audited. Revenue growth came from a whole team and market conditions. My contribution was the visibility, the database and the staffing model, not the whole result. Exact dollar figures are on my CV.

Why the code is not public

The production code and data belong to my employer. I rebuilt the same patterns on public data, where you can read and run everything:

Finance Mart explorer: a star schema over 1.07 million invoice lines, reconciled to the penny, with 32 tests (public practice data, not employer data).
HN Pulse: dbt models with 27 data tests that block a bad update from reaching the live dashboard.
